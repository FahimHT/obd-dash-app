obd-dash

An auxiliary gauge cluster for Android, fed by an ELM327 adapter.

A car's factory dash shows speed, revs and fuel. obd-dash shows what it leaves out: coolant temperature as an actual reading rather than a warning light, battery voltage, engine load, and the diagnostic trouble codes behind a check-engine lamp.

What it does

  • Reads generic OBD-II (SAE J1979) data over a classic-Bluetooth ELM327 adapter.
  • Draws gauges sized to be read at a glance, and raises an alert when a reading leaves its healthy band.
  • Records each ignition cycle as a trip — distance, fuel, economy, battery trend — and keeps the history on the phone.
  • Reads stored fault codes and freeze-frame data, and can clear them.

What it does not do

  • No account is required to use it.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no advertising.
  • The vehicle identification number is never requested from the car.

Backing the trip history up to Google Drive is optional and off until you turn it on. What that involves is set out in the privacy policy.

Requirements

  • Android 12 or newer.
  • An ELM327 Bluetooth adapter, paired with the phone.
  • A vehicle with an OBD-II port — generally petrol cars from 2001 and diesel from 2004.
