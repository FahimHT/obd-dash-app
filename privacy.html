Privacy policy

OBD Dash, by Owl Software · last updated 28 November 2026

Almost everything OBD Dash records stays on your phone, including the route of each drive. Three things leave it, and only if you choose them: backup to your own Google Drive, the phone's position, sent to the OBD Dash portal so you can see where your phone is, and, if you join the leaderboard, a summary of each trip, shown to other users as totals beside your display name. There is no account to create in order to use the app.

What the app records

While connected to your vehicle it reads standard OBD-II diagnostic data — engine speed, road speed, coolant temperature, battery voltage, engine load, fuel trims, intake air temperature and stored fault codes. From these it derives trip records: distance, fuel used, duration and the extremes each drive reached.

If you allow location, it also records each drive's route: the phone's position once a second during a drive, with the car's readings, even with the app closed if you allow location all the time. Routes are kept on your phone for your newest fifty drives and up to 25 you pin.

It writes diagnostic log files to the phone's own storage, to make faults reproducible after a drive. You can clear or export these at any time from Settings.

What it deliberately does not record

  • The vehicle identification number is never requested. The app does not ask the car for it, so it cannot appear in any record or export.
  • No location outside a drive. Routes are recorded only while a drive is being recorded. The portal (below) keeps the phone's latest position alone; each one replaces the last.
  • Log files are filtered as they are written, so a vehicle identification number, a pair of coordinates or the identifying half of a Bluetooth address cannot be recorded even accidentally.
  • There is no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking of any kind.

Permissions, and why each exists

  • Bluetooth — to connect to the ELM327 adapter. This is the only way the app reaches the vehicle.
  • Location — optional, asked for only after the app has explained what it is used for, and covering every use: the route of each drive, the satellite receiver's speed compared against the car's own speedometer, and the phone's position for the portal.
  • Location all the time — optional, asked for after you allow location, so a drive is recorded with the app closed, and the portal hears from a phone left in the car.
  • Notifications — optional, for the ongoing notification shown while the app watches for the car starting.
  • Network access — used only for Google Drive backup, the portal and the leaderboard, the leaderboard's Google profile photos, and the map a route is drawn on.

Google Drive backup

This is optional and off until you turn it on. When you do, the app asks for permission to use Google Drive and uploads a copy of your trip history and the vehicles you have set up, so the same history is available on your other phones.

  • The app requests the drive.file permission, which grants access only to files the app itself creates. It cannot see, read or alter anything else in your Drive.
  • The files are stored in your own Google account, under your own storage quota. The developer has no access to them and no copy of them.
  • The uploaded data is the same trip history the app already holds — dates, distances, fuel figures, battery readings and fault codes — the vehicle names you typed, and the route of each drive the phone keeps, one file per drive. It contains no identification number.
  • Diagnostic log files are never uploaded.

Export, on the History screen, saves the same history to a file you choose, routes included. Where that file goes is up to you.

The route map

A drive's route is drawn on a map from OpenFreeMap, built from OpenStreetMap data. To show the map, the app downloads map images for the area on screen, so OpenFreeMap sees which area is being viewed and your phone's internet address, as any website sees a visitor. The route itself is never sent: it is drawn over the map on the phone. No account or key is used, and nothing else is shared.

The OBD Dash portal

This is optional. When the app first asks for location it explains the portal, and nothing is sent unless you agree. When you do, the app sends the following to the portal every few minutes, including while the app is closed or not in use, so you can see where your phone is by signing in there with the same Google account:

  • the phone's latest position and how old it is;
  • an identifier for the phone (Android's ID for this app), the phone's name, and the version of OBD Dash it runs;
  • the name and email address of the Google account you chose in the app, which the portal confirms with Google at least once an hour so nobody can report under your account.
  • Where it is kept: in Owl Software's portal, a Cloudflare Worker with a Cloudflare D1 database. Only the latest report per phone is kept; there is no location history.
  • Who can see it: you, by signing in with the Google account the phone reports under; and Owl Software, who can see every phone's report — its precise last position, its name, the app version, and the account's name and email — in order to run and monitor the service, such as how many phones report, which devices, which have updated, and in which regions. It is not sold or shared with anyone else.
  • How long: until you delete it, or Owl Software removes it. A phone with location on reports again after a deletion; turn location off for the app to stop it.
  • Sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) only.

The leaderboard

This is optional and off until you join it in the app, under Settings, Leaderboard, which says what is sent before you do. It uses the Google account you chose in the app, and nothing is sent without it. When you join, the app sends to the OBD Dash portal:

  • the display name you pick, and, only if you turn it on, your Google profile photo, which the portal reads from Google itself;
  • for each trip from the last 365 days, and then each new trip once a day: an identifier for the trip and one for its car, both made on the phone so they carry no time or name, the date it started (the day, not the time), the distance and the fuel used. Never the time of day, the route, any position, or the car's name.
  • Who can see it: other people signed in to OBD Dash see your display name, your photo if you chose it, and four figures over the last 7, 30 and 365 days: fuel economy against your own car's usual, fuel saved, distance and drive days. The board shows the top ten of each, and each person their own place. It is not visible on the web. Owl Software can see every member's name and account email, to run the service and to hide a name somebody reports.
  • Reports: if you report a name, the portal keeps that your account reported it, until the name changes or either account leaves.
  • How long: each trip is kept for 90 days, then only its day's totals, for a year; nothing older is kept. Leaving deletes all of it at once.
  • Favorites you star, and names you hide for yourself, stay on your phone and are never sent.
  • Kept in the same Cloudflare Worker and D1 database as the portal, sent over HTTPS only, not sold or shared with anyone else.

Deleting your data

Everything the app records is on your phone and, if you turned on backup, in your own Google Drive. The copies Owl Software holds are the portal's and the leaderboard's, if you chose them. You delete all of it yourself, like this:

  1. One trip, and its route: History, open the trip, then Delete.
  2. One vehicle and everything recorded for it: Settings, Car, then Delete.
  3. All history on the phone: History, choose All cars, then Clear. Or uninstall the app, which removes everything it stored on the phone.
  4. The Google Drive backup: turn off Back up history to Google Drive under Settings, Sync; delete the obd-dash folder from your Google Drive; and remove the app's access in your Google account permissions.
  5. What the portal holds: in the app, Settings, Location, then Delete my portal data; or, without the app, at the portal's deletion page, signing in with the same Google account. Either removes every phone under your account, and also takes you off the leaderboard. A phone with location on reports again at its next check-in; turn location off for OBD Dash to stop it.
  6. The leaderboard: in the app, Settings, Leaderboard, then Leave. Your name, every trip sent and their totals are deleted at once.

What is kept: nothing. Deleted data is gone at once, and no other copy exists anywhere, and the portal keeps no record of the asking. Items deleted from Google Drive follow Drive's own trash rules until you empty it.

Children

The app is a vehicle instrument and is not directed at children. The only personal information it collects is what the portal and leaderboard sections above list, and only from somebody who agrees to it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. No change will make the app send data anywhere without asking you first.
